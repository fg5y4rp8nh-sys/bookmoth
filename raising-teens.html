3

Why Teens Pull Away

One of the hardest parts of parenting teenagers is feeling pushed away. The child who wanted to be with you now prefers friends, keeps their door shut, and seems irritated by your very existence.

It's their job. Psychologists describe adolescence as a period of individuation: becoming a separate individual with your own thoughts, values and life. To do that, teens need to step back from their parents. Sometimes they do it by criticizing you, disagreeing with you, or simply spending more time alone and with friends. It isn't a rejection of you as a person; it's a necessary step toward adulthood.

Friends become central. In adolescence, friends become a primary source of support, identity and belonging. This is normal and important. Teens are learning how to build relationships outside the family, which they'll need for the rest of their lives.
