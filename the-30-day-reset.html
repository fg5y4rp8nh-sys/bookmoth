How Habits Actually Work

A habit is a behavior that has become automatic: you do it in a particular situation without really deciding to. Brushing your teeth before bed. Checking your phone when you wake up. Reaching for a snack when you sit down to watch TV.

Research by the psychologist Wendy Wood and others suggests that a large share of our everyday behavior is habitual, carried out with little conscious thought. That's why willpower alone is such an unreliable way to change. Willpower is needed when you're making a decision. Habits take the decision out of it.

To build a new habit, you need a clear cue, an easy behavior and a satisfying reward. To break an old one, it helps to change or remove the cue.
