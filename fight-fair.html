1

What You're Really Fighting About

Almost every couple has had a fight that started with something tiny: a late reply, a wet towel on the bed, a forgotten errand. Twenty minutes later, both people are hurt and exhausted, and neither can remember how it got so bad.

Sue Johnson, the founder of Emotionally Focused Therapy, describes most couple conflict as a protest against disconnection. When we feel distant from a partner, we get scared, even if we don't recognize it as fear. That fear comes out as anger, criticism, sulking or shutting down. The fight looks like it's about the towel, but underneath, it's a question: Are you still there for me?

Here's an example. Maya texts her partner, Leo, at lunch. He doesn't reply until 9 p.m. When he gets home, she says coldly, "Must've been a busy day." He says, "It was, actually," and it's on.
