7

What Inflation Is (and Isn't)

Inflation is a general rise in prices across the economy, which means each dollar buys a little less than it used to.

The key word is general. If coffee gets expensive because of a bad harvest in Brazil, that's a change in one price, driven by supply and demand. Inflation is when prices are rising broadly: food, rent, cars, haircuts, and wages, all drifting up together.

Inflation is usually reported as a yearly percentage. If inflation is 3 percent, something that cost $100 a year ago now costs about $103 on average. That doesn't sound like much, but it adds up. At 3 percent a year, prices roughly double in about 24 years. (Chapter 23 explains a handy trick for working that out.)
