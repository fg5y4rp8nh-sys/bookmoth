Where Tarot Came From

Tarot didn't begin as a fortune-telling tool. It began as a card game.

The earliest surviving tarot cards come from northern Italy in the fifteenth century, painted for wealthy families like the Visconti and Sforza of Milan. They were used to play a trick-taking game called tarocchi, a bit like modern bridge, with an extra suit of picture cards, the "triumphs," that beat all the others. People in France, Austria, and other parts of Europe still play tarot card games today.

The link between tarot and mysticism came much later. In 1781, a French writer named Antoine Court de Gébelin claimed, without any real evidence, that the cards preserved secret wisdom from ancient Egypt. The idea caught on. Over the next century, French and British occultists connected the cards to astrology, the Hebrew alphabet, and the four classical elements, and began using them for divination.
